The short version

Free: Khan Academy official prep, College Board Bluebook practice tests, and Satified. Zero dollars, and this covers most students.

$249 to $399: self paced paid platforms such as UWorld.

$699 to $2,199: live online courses from Kaplan, PrepScholar, and The Princeton Review.

$1,600 to $10,000+: a full private tutoring engagement at roughly $70 an hour.

The honest take: price buys instruction hours and accountability, not better questions. The questions that matter most are official, and those are free.

The free tier, and why it is not a consolation prize

The single most important fact about SAT prep pricing is that the official questions are free. The College Board publishes real practice material through Khan Academy and ships full length adaptive practice tests inside the same Bluebook app you use on test day. Every paid course in the table above teaches around that same official material.

So the honest question is not "free or paid." It is "what does free actually leave missing." In practice there has been one real gap: drilling volume at medium and hard difficulty. Students consistently report running out of hard official questions well before test day, and repetition on the same handful of problems turns into memorizing answers rather than learning patterns.

That gap is the entire reason Satified exists. Every one of its 1,483 SAT Math questions is a generator rather than a static problem, so it rebuilds with fresh numbers each time you load it. You cannot exhaust it, and you cannot memorize your way through it.

When paying is actually worth it

This page is published by a free tool, so treat the following with appropriate suspicion and judge it on the reasoning. There are real situations where paying makes sense:

  • You will not do the work alone. This is the honest and most common reason. A $949 course buys a schedule, a room, and somebody expecting you. If self directed study has already failed you twice, that structure is worth real money.
  • You cannot diagnose your own weakness. If your score is stuck and you genuinely cannot tell why, a few hours with a good tutor to identify the pattern can be worth more than a hundred hours of unfocused drilling. Note that this is a few hours, not thirty.
  • You need the verbal section too. Satified is SAT Math only. If Reading and Writing is your weak half, you need something else, and Khan Academy covers it for free.
  • You are chasing the last 60 points. Going from 1500 to 1560 is a different problem from going from 1100 to 1300, and expert coaching is more defensible at the top of the range.

What paying does not reliably buy is better questions. The official ones are free, and no company has access to the real test.

The zero dollar study plan

If cost is the constraint, this is a complete plan that costs nothing:

  • Baseline. Take one full official practice test in Bluebook. You cannot plan without knowing your starting score and which section is dragging it.
  • Learn the concepts. Use Khan Academy for anything you got wrong because you did not know the material, on both sections.
  • Drill the patterns. Use Satified for Math volume, filtered to the difficulty tier and skill you are weakest in. When you get stuck, the free AI tutor explains the step rather than handing over the answer.
  • Simulate. Take adaptive practice tests under time so the two module structure feels routine.
  • Review every miss. This is the step people skip, and it is where the points are. A wrong answer you never diagnosed will be wrong again on test day.

That sequence covers diagnosis, instruction, practice volume, and simulation, which is exactly what a paid course charges several hundred dollars to package together.

SAT prep cost, answered

How much does SAT prep cost?
Anywhere from nothing to more than $10,000. Official practice on Khan Academy and in Bluebook is free, and so is Satified. Self paced paid platforms such as UWorld run roughly $249 to $399. Live online courses run roughly $699 to $2,199. Private tutoring averages about $70 an hour, and a full engagement commonly totals $1,600 to $10,000 or more.
Is free SAT prep good enough?
For most students, yes. The official question bank and the real Bluebook practice tests are the same material paid courses teach around. The gap free tools historically left was drilling volume at medium and hard difficulty, which is the specific gap Satified fills. Paying is worth considering when you need accountability or one on one diagnosis.
Is expensive SAT prep better?
Not automatically. Price mostly buys live instruction hours and accountability rather than better questions, and the questions that matter most are official and free. A motivated student drilling consistently with free tools regularly outscores a student who paid for a course and did not do the work.
How much does a private SAT tutor cost per hour?
Commonly $45 to $100 an hour, averaging near $70. Experienced professionals run about $75 to $125, agency tutors $125 to $300 or more, and elite specialists $200 to $500. Online sessions typically cost 10 to 25 percent less than in home ones.
Why is Satified free?
It was built by one high school student who could not justify the price of the paid options, and it stays free because cost should never be the reason a student cannot prepare. No paid tier, no account, no ads.
What is the cheapest way to prep for the SAT?
Spend nothing. Baseline in Bluebook, learn concepts on Khan Academy, drill weak skills at real difficulty on Satified, then simulate under time. That covers everything a paid course packages together.
