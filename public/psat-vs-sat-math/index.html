PSAT vs SAT Math: Same Test, Different Scale

If you are deciding how to study for the PSAT, here is the honest version. The PSAT/NMSQT Math section and the SAT Math section are built to the same blueprint, and the one difference that really matters is the score scale, not the content. So almost all of your prep counts for both, and you do not need two separate plans.

The short answer

The PSAT/NMSQT Math section and the SAT Math section share almost everything that defines a test. Same number of questions, same timing, same two module shape, same four content domains. According to College Board, each one runs 44 questions across two 35 minute modules, and both adapt the second module to how you did on the first.

The difference that changes your plan is the score scale. PSAT Math is reported from 160 to 760, and SAT Math is reported from 200 to 800. That is the reason you cannot copy a PSAT number onto an SAT goal and call it settled. Everything else is close enough that practice for one is practice for the other. In plain terms, if you can do SAT Math you can do PSAT Math, and the reverse is nearly true as well.

What is the same

Start with what does not change, because it is most of the test.

The question count matches. Both sections give you 44 Math questions, no more and no fewer. The timing matches too, 70 minutes split into two 35 minute modules, and both use adaptive routing, so the difficulty of your second module depends on how the first one went.

The content matches. Both pull from the same four SAT Suite Math domains: Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry. You can browse those SAT Math topics and trust that every one of them is fair game on the PSAT as well as the SAT.

The tools match. Both tests let you use a calculator on every question, and both build the Desmos graphing calculator right into the testing app. If you learn when to reach for Desmos and when to work by hand for the SAT, that judgment carries straight to the PSAT with nothing to relearn.

What is different

Three things actually differ, and only one of them changes how you should study.

The score scale is the real difference. PSAT Math runs from 160 to 760, while SAT Math runs from 200 to 800. The totals move the same way, since the PSAT/NMSQT tops out at 1520 on a 320 to 1520 scale and the SAT tops out at 1600 on a 400 to 1600 scale. When you set a goal, keep the two scales apart. A 700 on the PSAT and a 700 on the SAT are not the same result, and our SAT score calculator is built on the SAT scale, so do not feed it a PSAT target and expect a matching read.

The difficulty is a little different. The PSAT/NMSQT is aimed at earlier grade students, so its questions tend to sit at grade appropriate levels that are slightly easier on average. The skill names and the question formats still match the SAT exactly. You are not learning different math, you are seeing the same math without the very hardest end of the SAT range.

The purpose is different. The PSAT/NMSQT is practice, and it is the entry point to the National Merit Scholarship Program. The SAT is the test colleges use for admissions. That is why so many students sit the PSAT first and treat it as a low stakes rehearsal for the exam that counts.

One note on National Merit

If National Merit is your reason for caring about the PSAT, remember that your Math score alone does not decide it. The program uses the Selection Index, a combined number drawn from every section of the PSAT/NMSQT, not the Math score on its own. Strong Math helps, but a balanced score across the whole test is what lifts the Selection Index. Practice Math hard, and do not neglect the rest.

Questions students ask

Is PSAT Math the same as SAT Math?
Almost. Both have 44 questions in two 35 minute modules and the same four domains. The main difference is the score scale: PSAT Math runs 160 to 760, SAT Math runs 200 to 800.
Is PSAT Math easier than SAT Math?
Slightly. The PSAT targets earlier grade students, so questions sit at grade appropriate levels, but the skills and formats are the same.
Can I use SAT Math practice for the PSAT?
Yes. The skills overlap so heavily that SAT Math practice covers almost all of PSAT Math. Treat the hardest SAT questions as stretch practice.
Does the PSAT count for college?
Not for admissions. The PSAT/NMSQT is practice and the entry point to National Merit. Colleges use the SAT.
How do PSAT and SAT Math scores compare?
They use different scales, so do not treat a PSAT number as an SAT number. PSAT Math tops out at 760, SAT Math at 800.
