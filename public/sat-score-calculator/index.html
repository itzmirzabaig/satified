How digital SAT Math scoring works

The Math section gives you 44 questions in two 35 minute modules of 22. Module 1 mixes easy, medium, and hard questions, and how you do there decides which module 2 you get: a harder set that keeps the full 200 to 800 scale in play, or an easier set that public analyses say caps somewhere around the high 600s. Your scaled score then comes from both modules together, weighted by question characteristics.

Mechanism 01

Two modules, one fork

Do well in module 1 and the test routes you to a harder module 2. Struggle and it routes you to an easier one. College Board confirms the second module adapts to the first, and that fork is the single biggest variable in any conversion.

Mechanism 02

Difficulty weighs in

Your score reflects which questions you got right, not just how many. College Board says two students with the same raw total can earn different section scores because their correct answers differed in difficulty.

Mechanism 03

Hidden pretest questions

College Board seeds two unscored pretest questions into each module, and nothing marks them. You can never know the exact point value of any one question you saw, so exact per question math is a fiction.

Mechanism 04

A real ceiling on the easy path

Prep analyses put the easier module 2 ceiling somewhere around 600 to 700, and College Board says a couple of module 1 misses can put an 800 out of reach even with a perfect module 2. Module 1 accuracy buys your ceiling.

Why every score calculator is an estimate

College Board explains the machinery but never publishes the numbers. There is no official raw to scaled table for the adaptive digital SAT, and its own paper practice guides prove how much conversions move: across the official scoring guides for practice tests 4 through 11, the same raw score maps to different scaled ranges from form to form, with a Math raw score of 40 landing anywhere from 570 to 640 depending on the test. Those guides also describe a longer paper form, so their tables cannot be copied straight onto the 44 question digital section.

The routing threshold is inferred too. Prep companies converge on roughly 14 or 15 correct out of 22 in module 1 for the harder path, but College Board has never confirmed a number. Some calculators still return a single exact score. That is more precision than the public evidence supports, which is why this one blends the official ranges with the reported path ceilings, returns a low and a high, states its assumptions, and tells you when a combination of raw score and path is not even possible.

Questions students ask

How accurate is an SAT Math score calculator?
Any public calculator is an estimate. College Board does not publish a complete raw to scaled conversion for the adaptive digital SAT, and official scores depend on question difficulty, module routing, and answer pattern, not just your total correct. This tool shows a range and states its assumptions instead of pretending to be exact.
Can two students with the same raw score get different Math scores?
Yes. College Board says two students with the same number of correct answers can earn different section scores, because the questions they answered correctly can differ in difficulty and other characteristics.
How many module 1 questions do I need to reach the harder module 2?
College Board does not publish the routing threshold. Independent prep analyses converge on roughly 14 or 15 correct out of 22 in module 1, and this calculator assumes 15 or more for the harder path.
Can I still score 800 if I got the easier module 2?
Almost certainly not. Public analyses put the easier path ceiling somewhere around 600 to 700, and College Board itself says that missing a couple of module 1 questions can make an 800 section score impossible even with a perfect module 2.
Do all 44 Math questions count toward my score?
No. College Board's scoring documentation lists two pretest questions per module that do not count, and you cannot tell which ones they were. That is one more reason an exact raw to scaled conversion is impossible from the outside.

Yes. Free, no account, no email. The practice drills behind it are free too, so the estimate can turn into a plan on the same site.
