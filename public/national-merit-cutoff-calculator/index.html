How the National Merit cutoff works

The PSAT/NMSQT is the entry point to the National Merit Scholarship Program, which is run by the National Merit Scholarship Corporation, an independent organization. About 1.5 million students enter, and roughly the top 50,000 by Selection Index are recognized. Of those, around 34,000 are named Commended Students and about 16,000 become Semifinalists.

There are two thresholds to know:

  • Commended: a single national number, recently around 207 to 209. Students at or above it who do not make Semifinalist are usually named Commended.
  • Semifinalist: set separately for each state, so the bar in a high scoring state is much higher than in a low scoring one. Recent state cutoffs have run from about 207 to about 225.

Recent state Semifinalist cutoffs

These are reported estimates for a recent class, gathered by prep industry trackers. They are not official, and they move every year, so treat them as a rough guide, not a promise.

If you are near the cutoff, Math is often the fastest lever

Selection Index points are the whole game, and Math is frequently where they are easiest to find, because SAT and PSAT Math reward pattern recognition that responds quickly to focused practice. Since the PSAT and SAT Math sections are nearly identical, the same drilling works for both. If you are within a few points of your state cutoff, a targeted Math push before your next PSAT can be the difference.

Questions students ask

What is the National Merit Selection Index?
It is two times your PSAT Reading and Writing score plus your Math score, divided by 10. It ranges from 48 to 228, and it is what National Merit uses, not your total PSAT score.
What is the Commended Student cutoff?
In recent years it has landed around 207 to 209 nationally. It is a single national number set each year, and students at or above it who are not Semifinalists are usually named Commended.
What Selection Index do I need to be a Semifinalist?
It depends on your state. Recent state cutoffs have run from about 207 in the lowest states to about 225 in the highest, and they change every year.
Does Math or Reading and Writing matter more for National Merit?
Reading and Writing counts twice in the index and Math counts once, so a Reading and Writing gain moves the index more per point. Math is still often where the fastest available points are.
Are these National Merit cutoffs official?
No. State cutoffs are reported estimates from prep industry trackers and vary year to year. Use them as a guide and confirm with official National Merit Scholarship Corporation sources.
