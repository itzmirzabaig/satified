What is the SAT?

The SAT is a digital college admissions test from the College Board, scored from 400 to 1600. It has two sections, Reading and Writing and Math, and it is used by many US colleges as one piece of an application. Here is how the test is built, how it is scored, and how to get ready without paying for it.

The short answer

The SAT is a standardized test that most US colleges accept as part of admissions. It is written and run by the College Board, and since 2024 it is taken entirely on a computer through the College Board's Bluebook app rather than on paper. It measures the reading, writing, and math skills that colleges treat as a rough common yardstick across very different high schools.

A total SAT score runs from 400 to 1600. Colleges read it alongside your grades, courses, essays, and everything else, so it is one signal among many, not a verdict. And because scores can be raised with practice, it is one of the few parts of an application you can still move in the months before test day.

The two sections

The digital SAT has just two sections, each scored from 200 to 800. Add them together and you get your total out of 1600.

  • Reading and Writing. Short passages, each followed by a single question. It covers reading comprehension, vocabulary in context, sentence structure, grammar, and how evidence supports a claim. There are 54 questions.
  • Math. Covers algebra, advanced math, problem solving and data analysis, and geometry and trigonometry. Roughly 75 percent is multiple choice and the rest are student produced responses, where you type your own answer. There are 44 questions, and a calculator is allowed on all of them.

How the digital SAT works

The biggest change from the old paper test is that the digital SAT is section-adaptive. Each of the two sections is split into two shorter parts called modules:

  • Everyone takes the first module of a section, which has a mix of easy, medium, and hard questions.
  • How you do on that first module decides whether your second module is the easier set or the harder set. Reaching the harder second module is what puts the top of the score range within reach.

A few things that make the digital format easier to sit than the old one: reading passages are short, with one question each instead of long shared passages; there is a countdown timer you can hide; you can flag questions and come back; and the built in Desmos graphing calculator is available for the entire Math section. Scores also come back in a matter of days rather than weeks.

How the SAT is scored

Your total score is simply the two section scores added together:

  • Reading and Writing: 200 to 800
  • Math: 200 to 800
  • Total: 400 to 1600

There is no penalty for wrong answers, so you should never leave a question blank, even if it is a guess. Raw scores, the number of questions you get right, are converted to the 200 to 800 scale in a way that accounts for which module you reached. For context, the average total score is around 1050. Above 1200 is competitive for a lot of schools, and the most selective colleges commonly see 1400 and up. The number that matters most is the middle 50 percent range published by the specific colleges on your list.

Who takes it, and when

Most students take the SAT in the spring of junior year and again in the fall of senior year, which leaves room to raise the score the second time. It is offered on set national dates several times a year, and you register through the College Board and pick a test center and date.

The PSAT is the SAT's shorter warm up, usually taken in the fall. It does not go to colleges, but a strong PSAT can qualify you for National Merit recognition and scholarship money. The SAT, answered

What is the SAT?
A standardized college admissions test from the College Board, now fully digital and taken on the Bluebook app. It measures reading, writing, and math, is scored from 400 to 1600, and is used by many US colleges as one part of an application.
How is the SAT scored?
The total runs from 400 to 1600, the sum of two section scores that each range from 200 to 800, one for Reading and Writing and one for Math. There is no penalty for wrong answers, so answer every question.
How long is the SAT?
About 2 hours and 14 minutes of testing time, not counting breaks. Reading and Writing is 64 minutes and Math is 70 minutes.
What sections are on the SAT?
Two: Reading and Writing, and Math. Each is split into two shorter modules, for 98 scored questions in total, 54 in Reading and Writing and 44 in Math.
Is the SAT adaptive now?
Yes. It is section-adaptive. Each section has two modules, and how you do on the first decides whether the second is easier or harder. Both modules set your section score.
Can you use a calculator on the SAT?
Yes, on the entire Math section. Bluebook has a built in Desmos graphing calculator for every math question, and you may also bring your own approved calculator.
What is a good SAT score?
It depends on your colleges. The average is around 1050, above 1200 is competitive for many schools, and the most selective colleges often see 1400 and up. Check the middle 50 percent range for the schools on your list.
