What the SAT Desmos calculator is

On the digital SAT, the College Board gives every student an embedded Desmos graphing calculator inside Bluebook, the official testing app. It is a full graphing calculator with a scientific mode, and you can toggle between them. It is available on the entire Math section, so there is no separate no calculator part the way the old paper SAT had.

Because it is built into the test, the smartest thing you can do is get comfortable with it before test day. The calculator on this page is the real Desmos engine, so anything you learn here transfers directly to Bluebook.

What to graph, and what to solve by hand

Desmos is a weapon when you use it in the right spots and a time sink when you graph everything. A quick rule:

  • Graph it: systems of equations (the intersection is the solution), quadratics and other nonlinear equations, function behavior, and inequalities.
  • Use a table for: testing answer choices, finding function values, and checking a sequence of numbers.
  • Solve by hand: simple linear equations, percent change, and basic geometry formulas, where opening a graph is slower than just doing it.

The full breakdown, with examples, is in the Desmos strategy guide, and the Desmos plus AI review method shows how to pair it with the tutor when you get a question wrong.

Questions students ask

Is there a Desmos calculator on the SAT?
Yes. An embedded Desmos graphing calculator is built into Bluebook, the digital SAT testing app, and it is available throughout the entire Math section.
Can I use the calculator on the whole Math section?
Yes. The digital SAT allows calculator use on every Math question. There is no separate no calculator section like the old paper SAT had.
Do I have to use Desmos?
No. You can also bring an approved handheld calculator, and many questions are faster by hand. Desmos is there for when graphing or testing values is quicker.
Can I practice with the SAT Desmos calculator before test day?
Yes, and you should. The calculator on this page is free to use, and the same Desmos is built into every question in Satified's practice app, so you learn it in context.
What should I graph versus solve by hand?
Graph systems of equations, intersections, and function behavior. Solve simple linear equations, percent changes, and basic formulas by hand, since opening a graph for those is slower.
